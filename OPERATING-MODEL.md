# Operating Model — Frozen

## Four Workstreams, Asymmetric Investment

```
Research (40%)
      ↓
HPF (30%)
      ↓
Books (20%)
      ↓
Public Content (10%)
```

Each feeds the next. Never try to "finish" one before starting another.

---

## Execution Cycle

Not a calendar. Think in **cycles**, not weekdays. A cycle completes when all downstream outputs are updated, regardless of how many calendar days it takes.

| Phase | Workstream | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Research | Research dossier for one stable domain |
| 2 | Canonical + HPF | Canonical concepts → HPF knowledge objects |
| 3 | HPF | One M2 improvement cycle (evidence → change → benchmark) |
| 4 | Books + Public | Update all 3 outline trees + publish one public artifact |

One cycle feeds everything.

---

## Research (40%)

Highest-leverage work. Every hour compounds across all downstream outputs.

**Process**: Research dossier → canonical concept → HPF object → books → blog → course.

**Scope**: One stable domain at a time (Browser State, Browser Memory, Browser Perception, etc.). Technology profiles are derived, not researched independently.

## HPF (30%)

**Process**: Benchmark → one behavioural deficiency → single change → re-benchmark.

No feature work outside the evidence loop. Only one change per cycle for attribution.

## Books (20%)

**Process**: Maintain three outline trees, not three manuscripts. Each researched domain produces a note in all three outlines. Serious writing begins only after 60-70% of domains are mature.

Books are consumers of HPF knowledge, not competitors for time.

## Public Content (10%)

Every completed concept generates something public: blog post, LinkedIn article, diagram, demo, talk, or GitHub note. Builds credibility before books are finished.

---

## What NOT to Do

- Do not finish HPF before starting books
- Do not finish Book 1 before starting Book 2
- Do not research "for Book 1" — research domains, not books
- Do not make multi-change HPF cycles — no attribution
- Do not write polished prose before 60-70% research maturity
