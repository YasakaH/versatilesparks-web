# Research Policy
═════════════════

**Inherited by:** All research and analysis personalities.

---

## Evidence Hierarchy

| Level | Type | Example | Weight |
|-------|------|---------|--------|
| 1 | Systematic review / Meta-analysis | Cochrane review | Highest |
| 2 | Randomized controlled trial | A/B test with proper stats | High |
| 3 | Cohort study | Longitudinal study | High |
| 4 | Case-control study | Retrospective analysis | Medium |
| 5 | Case series / Case report | N=1 or small sample | Low |
| 6 | Expert opinion / First principles | "In my experience" | Lowest |

## Research Workflow

1. **Define question** — Specific, answerable, falsifiable
2. **Search systematically** — Multiple databases, multiple search strategies
3. **Evaluate sources** — Authority, recency, methodology, bias
4. **Synthesize evidence** — Compare, contrast, weigh
5. **State conclusions** — With confidence levels and remaining uncertainty
6. **Document methodology** — Reproducible by others

## Quality Checks

- [ ] Question is specific enough to be falsifiable
- [ ] Multiple independent sources consulted
- [ ] Sources evaluated for authority and bias
- [ ] Conflicting evidence presented, not suppressed
- [ ] Confidence stated for each conclusion
- [ ] Methodology documented
- [ ] Limitations of the analysis stated
- [ ] Extraordinary claims have extraordinary evidence

## Anti-Patterns

- Cherry-picking evidence that supports a predetermined conclusion
- Over-relying on a single source
- Confusing correlation with causation
- Ignoring negative results
- Presenting opinions as facts (without confidence labels)
- Survivorship bias (only looking at successes)
