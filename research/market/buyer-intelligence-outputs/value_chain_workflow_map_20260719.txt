# KEY FINDINGS

## 1. The market is forming fast — but raw

- "Field went from zero to ~10 funded entrants in a year" (bjro.dev, Apr 2026)
- Corgi: $160M Series B at $1.3B valuation (May 2026)
- Armilla: $25M raised, Lloyd's-backed MGA
- Mount: YC P26, 2-person team, hiring founding engineer for underwriting engine
- Klaimee: YC, risk scoring + certification
- First policy written: ElevenLabs (Feb 2026)

## 2. Every carrier is building their underwriting engine from scratch

Mount's job posting:
  "Build the MVP underwriting engine used to score and price AI risk"
  "Design the data flows, heuristics, and product logic"
  "Existing carriers either exclude AI risk, price it badly, or avoid it
   entirely because they do not know how to assess deployed AI systems"

This confirms: NO standardized trace data format exists. Every carrier
builds their own data pipeline from proprietary framework traces.

## 3. The missing primitive is identified by the industry

Blog post by Björn Roberg (Apr 2026):

  "What's still missing is narrower and sharper: Record → Replay → Expire"
  "Record a session: inputs, outputs, side-effect evidence, timestamps"
  "Replay that recording as a conformance test against the live target"
  "Expire: the bundle carries a TTL — past expiry it's stale"
  "That is the only attestation shape that means anything"

This is EXACTLY the EPR format we built. The blog describes:
  - Record: our Event model + receipt
  - Replay: our diff engine + viewer
  - Expire: TTL-bound attestation (not yet built)

## 4. The two possible business paths

Blog identifies two forks:

  (a) "Substrate carries insurance" — You underwrite risk directly
      → Bigger company, harder, needs insurance partner
      → Klaimee is the only attempt

  (b) "Stay in audit lane" — Attestation that auditors accept
      → Smaller business, faster to build, acquirable
      → Where most current cohort sits

Our opportunity: Layer 3 infrastructure doesn't pick a side.
It serves BOTH paths. Carriers (a) and auditors (b) both need
standardized, verifiable execution evidence.

## 5. The workflow map (reconstructed from evidence)

Current state (manual, ad-hoc):

  Agent causes incident
    ↓
  Customer exports logs from multiple frameworks
    ↓
  Security engineer reconstructs timeline manually
    ↓
  Different trace formats from different frameworks
    ↓
  Claims analyst correlates tool calls
    ↓
  Manual decision: covered or excluded?
    ↓
  Payout (weeks later)

  "The first audits may be too manual. The first claims may be painful." (LinkedIn)

## 6. What infrastructure would remove

  Manual trace collection across frameworks → automated EPR receipts
  Manual timeline reconstruction → standardized event sequence
  Manual claims investigation → verifiable evidence bundle
  Disputed claims → replayable execution record

## 7. The killer question from the blog

  "Does EVERY insurer reconstruct execution history DIFFERENTLY?"
  → Yes. Every carrier builds their own pipeline from scratch.
  → This is exactly how FICO-like infrastructure emerges.

  "If someone ships [Record → Replay → Expire] as boring open-source
   infrastructure... Stage 1 is infrastructure, not business."
  → The author explicitly calls for someone to build this layer.
